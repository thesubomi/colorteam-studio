The challenge

A global catalogue that had to feel local

Netflix was releasing a steady wave of Nigerian and international titles. Each one had to feel relevant, timely and worth talking about, on four platforms with different rules.

  1. Cultural localisationGlobal titles had to feel local without bending the Netflix brand.
  2. Platform-native formatsEach platform rewards a different shape of post.
  3. Always onA rhythm between launches, not just around them.
01 · Localisation

Global titles, spoken in Pidgin

Money Heist recut with a Pidgin dub for Nigerian fans. Jingle Jangle retold as "This na di tory of Jeronicus Jangle." Nigerian originals introduced the way fans talk: Lísàbí's cast, one character card at a time, so a Yoruba epic felt familiar before episode one.

02 · Native formats

One title, many native formats

Hijack '93 became passport cards and a pick-your-character grid. Òlòtūré became a swipeable fact file. Tokunbo, an annotated actor spotlight. Each format built for how people use that platform.

03 · Always-on engine

Always on, between launches

A shared calendar with Netflix's team mixed monthly roundups, reactive edits and brand moments: What We Watched, Now Playing, quote cards, meme edits, Woman Crush Wednesday, Season's Greetings.

Results

From under 10K to 600K+ Instagram followers

Four localised assets a week built an audience that kept coming back to the catalogue. Paid subscribers in Nigeria grew 5.5 times over the engagement, and more than 50 titles were promoted.
